Unmanned Subterranean Vehicles with Onboard Manipulator

VINIF-funded project on six-wheeled unmanned subterranean vehicles with an onboard manipulator, robust guidance-and-control, and 3D underground mapping for hazardous mine exploration

Development of unmanned subterranean vehicles with an onboard manipulator for underground environment exploration

  • Duration: Dec. 2025 – Present
  • Funding source: Vingroup Innovation Foundation (VINIF)
  • Project number: VINIF.2025.DA096
  • Role: Scientific Secretary
  • Announcement: VINIF project page
  • Patent: Self-Balancing Electric Wheelchair System Integrated with Piezoelectric Sensors

This industry-funded project designs and fabricates six-wheeled unmanned subterranean vehicles (USVs) with an onboard manipulator, a 6WD/6WS rocker-bogie guidance-and-control architecture, and a real-time 3D mapping and wireless sensor network for autonomous, fault-tolerant exploration of hazardous, GPS-denied underground environments. As scientific secretary, I coordinate team communication, track milestones and deliverables, and support technical documentation and progress reporting alongside the PI.

  1. S2
    Cascade Fixed-Time Prescribed-Performance Control for High-Speed Autonomous Vehicle Trajectory Tracking with Actuator Faults and Input Delay
    Tien Dung Nguyen, Duc Thinh Le, Duc Chinh Hoang, and 1 more author
    2026
    Manuscript submitted for publication (submitted 21 July 2026).
    Tien Dung Nguyen and Duc Thinh Le contributed equally as co-first authors.
    Abs Video

    Existing trajectory tracking controllers for autonomous vehicles (AVs) typically treat the steering angle as an ideal, directly available input, overlooking the actuation errors introduced by the electric power steering (EPS) system under real-world faults and delays. This paper proposes a fixed-time prescribed-performance trajectory tracking control framework for autonomous vehicles operating at high speeds under strong crosswinds, actuator faults, and steering input delays. To capture the interaction between vehicle motion and steering actuation, an integrated dynamic model combining the lateral vehicle dynamics and the electric power steering system is considered. The proposed cascade framework consists of an inner loop for electric power steering regulation and an outer loop for lateral trajectory tracking. Fixed-time extended state observers are developed in both loops to estimate unmeasured velocities and lumped disturbances caused by model uncertainties, strong crosswinds, actuator faults, and input delays. To improve transient performance during high-speed operation, a prescribed performance function is incorporated into the outer loop to constrain tracking errors within predefined bounds. Fixed-time sliding-mode controllers are then designed to ensure that the closed-loop tracking errors converge within a settling time independent of the initial conditions. Theoretical analysis establishes the fixed-time stability and prescribed-performance properties of the closed-loop system. MATLAB/Simulink and CarSim co-simulations under high-speed driving, crosswind disturbances, EPS actuator faults, and input delays show that the proposed method reduces the integral-error tracking metrics (IAE, ISE, ITAE) by more than 25% across all scenarios compared with two existing backstepping-based controllers, with substantially larger gains observed under certain conditions, confirming its rapid response, accurate tracking, and strong robustness.

  1. S1
    Robust Fixed-time Fault Tolerant Control for Nonlinear Full-car Active Suspension System
    Gia Khiem Dinh, Huu Viet Nguyen, Duc Chinh Hoang, and 2 more authors
    2026
    Manuscript submitted for publication (submitted 22 April 2026).
    Co-author.
    Abs

    This paper is an investment to develop a robust fixed-time fault-tolerant control for a seven DoF full-car active suspension system subjected to unknown nonlinearity, parameter uncertainties, and actuator faults. Although unknown dynamic and fault problems are well-known to usually exist in the dynamic model of the active suspension system on vehicles, solving for both issues simultaneously significantly increases the complexity of the control design. Moreover, the maximum suspension deflection constraint is discussed. To address this problem, a fixed-time disturbance observer is employed to accurately compensate for unknown dynamics and actuator faults in real-time. A nonlinear low-pass filter is proposed to generate the desired reference for the displacement of the vehicle body, which manages the tradeoffs between handling stability and riding comfort. By using a fixed-time control scheme to guarantee the tracking error convergence within a fixed time, the maximum suspension deflection constraint is ensured while successfully achieving ride comfort. The stability of the closed-loop system is demonstrated by the Lyapunov theory. Numerical simulation results are presented to verify the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method.
